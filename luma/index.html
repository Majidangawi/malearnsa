03What we're proposing

Three things, in the order we'd do them

We're applying to the Creative Partner Program, and we think the Education Program is the better-fitting half of the conversation. Either way, the work below happens.

1

Teach it

Luma becomes a taught module inside our live cohorts and our community classroom — not a mention, a curriculum unit with assignments and critique. Students leave having shipped something real with Ray and Uni-1, in Arabic, on their own client work.

2

Document it in Arabic

We keep publishing what we already publish for free — prompting guides, lesson recordings, workflow breakdowns — as Luma ships. The Uni-1 guide and the lesson above are the template. Attribution as your program requires it.

3

Put it in front of people

Our own work made with Luma, published on our channels with clear attribution, plus a free public masterclass on the platform. Awareness that comes from finished work rather than announcements.

04Why us, specifically

The overlap

Same market
Luma opened a Riyadh office to accelerate HUMAIN Create and is training on Arabic language and regional data. We are already in that market, in that language, with a paying Arabic-speaking creative audience — and we were teaching Uni-1 here before this conversation started.
Same buyer
Your MENA partnership runs through Publicis Groupe Middle East — agency creative and production teams. Our students and consulting clients are the people inside and around those teams: art directors, retouchers, producers, studio owners.
Craft first
Sixteen years in the creative industry before any of this — editorial and luxury campaign work for Boucheron, Chaumet, Bulgari, Graff, Dior, Prada, Gucci, Vogue Arabia and Harper's Bazaar Arabia. We teach the direction, and treat the model as the instrument.
Proven programs
We've run a brand ambassadorship before — Fujifilm Saudi Arabia, since 2024. We know what a partner program actually asks of a creator, and we deliver against it without being chased.
Language
Everything we make is Arabic-native — written in Saudi dialect for working creatives, not translated from English. That is the layer an Arabic-native model needs on top of it, and it is the layer almost nobody is building.
